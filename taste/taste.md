# Taste

Taste is the durable judgment layer for this workspace. Replace these neutral defaults with specific observations from work you admire and products you have designed.

## North star

Build useful product instruments: clear enough to understand quickly, precise enough for repeated expert use, and calm enough that the work remains primary.

## Product feel

- Dense, not crowded.
- Minimal, not empty.
- Refined, not precious.
- Systematic, not sterile.
- Familiar by default; novel when the benefit is visible.

## Interface values

1. The first screen should be the real tool whenever possible.
2. One screen should have one primary job.
3. Core objects and their state should be visible and manipulable.
4. Empty states should create momentum.
5. Typography should be quiet, sharp, and readable.
6. Motion should explain state or continuity, never decorate uncertainty.
7. Color should communicate hierarchy, state, or meaning.
8. Utility screens deserve the same craft as showcase screens.
9. Accessibility and performance are part of polish.
10. Specific values and behaviors beat vague adjectives.

## Critique questions

- What is the primary object and action?
- Can a new user predict what happens next?
- Is the hierarchy earned by information value?
- Which details feel generic rather than product-specific?
- Are important states missing or hidden?
- What can be removed without breaking the work?

## Avoid

- Decorative dashboards without an operational job.
- Generic AI gradients and glow used in place of hierarchy.
- Oversized marketing typography inside product tools.
- Pill, card, and shadow overload.
- Screens that explain themselves instead of becoming clearer.
- Treating a static mockup as final when interaction is the question.

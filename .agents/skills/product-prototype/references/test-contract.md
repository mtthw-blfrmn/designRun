# Prototype test contract

## Define before building

- Question: one uncertainty the prototype can reduce.
- Actor and situation: who is acting, with what prior context.
- Critical transition: the moment that produces evidence.
- Fidelity choice: why narrative, wireflow, coded behavior, or high visual fidelity is necessary.
- Observation: what will be watched or measured.
- Decision rule: what finding would preserve, revise, or stop the direction.
- Non-claims: what the prototype cannot establish.

## Simulation boundary

Mark sample data, mocked latency, fake services, generated content, disabled destructive actions, and any difference from production. A simulated success must never appear to be persisted, sent, purchased, published, or authorized. Use realistic edge data without real personal or customer content.

## Minimum coherent slice

Include entry, the critical action, system feedback, outcome, and recovery from the most consequential plausible failure. Add other states only when they can change the decision. Preserve enough object/state continuity for a participant to understand what changed.

## Evidence record

Record participant or reviewer context, build/version, task, observation, quote or event evidence, failure, interpretation, confidence, and resulting decision separately. Prototype polish is not evidence that the product direction is correct.
